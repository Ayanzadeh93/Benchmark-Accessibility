- Post-training quantization of tiny (<5M) hybrid conv/attention models. Naive W8A8 PTQ collapses because of highly dynamic per-channel activation ranges and zero-point overflow when channel-wise asymmetric quantization meets all-positive (post-ReLU) activations. [medium] — Q-HyViT: Post-Training Quantization of Hybrid Vision Transformers with Bridge Block Reconstruction for IoT Systems (2023/2024), arXiv:2303.12557. Evidence: ImageNet top-1. - Structure-preserving PTQ for depth: attention-preserving calibration of Q/K quantizers plus channel-wise affine distribution alignment (folded into weights after calibration); calibrates against a SILog loss between FP and quantized depth [medium] — SPA-Q: Structure-Preserving Adaptive Post-Training Quantization for Monocular Depth Estimation, arXiv:2611.04491 (Nov 2026). ### metric-scale
- Decoupled global metric-scale head: keep a scale/shift-invariant (relative) geometry branch, and predict one log-scale scalar from a global token with a 2-layer MLP. Train it with an L2 loss on log s* against the per-image optimal alignment scale between the relative prediction and GT, with stop-gradient so scale errors never backpropagate into the structure branch. [high] — MoGe-2: Accurate Monocular Geometry with Metric Scale and Sharp Details (Wang et al., NeurIPS 2025), arXiv:2507.02546. Evidence: ViT-B ablation, averaged over 10 datasets, Rel/delta1. Relative depth also improves: 9.83/89.0 for entangled SI-log vs 8.23/91.0 for decoupled. The paper reports that the entangled variant was unstable, because 'inaccurate scale predictions can produce large gradients that interfere with relative geometry learning'. A global MLP head beat a convolutional scale head, especially on metric geometry.. Inference cost: small (GAP over the last encoder stage plus a 2-layer MLP; tens of k params, negligible latency; Core ML friendly). Applicability: This is the most direct fit for your 'structure fine (d1_ssi ~0.93), scale wrong (raw ~0.66)' pattern. For B20: train the FPN output with an SSI/shift-invariant log-depth loss plus gradient matching. Add GAP(stage-4 features, optionally concatenated with an fx/fy embedding) -> MLP -> log s. Supervise it with stopgrad(log s*), where s* is the least-squares (or median) ratio between GT/teacher depth and the student's relative map. Output = exp(log s) * relative map. This replaces the entangled SILog training your S-series uses now. Your scale/scale2 losses only added scale penalties on top of an entangled output, which is a different design.

- 'Patient and consistent' distillation: a fixed (precomputed) teacher overfits; consistent views plus long schedules win [medium] — Knowledge distillation: A good teacher is patient and consistent, Beyer et al., CVPR 2022, arXiv:2106.05237 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.05237). Evidence: ImageNet ResNet-50 at 9600 epochs: fixed teacher (precomputed targets) 76.95%, consistent teaching 81.76%, function matching 82.31% (headline best run 82.8%). The fixed teacher peaks at 78.11% around 1200 epochs and then overfits (it starts degrading after roughly 600 epochs). Function matching at 1200 epochs (81.54%) equals consistent teaching at 4800 epochs. Long schedules keep paying off: Flowers102 student goes from 77.5% at 1k epochs to 96.9% at 1M epochs. All results are classification.. Inference cost: zero (train-only). Applicability: An offline cached teacher is their 'fixed teacher' failure mode, but for dense depth it is largely avoidable. Geometric augmentations (random-resized crop, flip, scale, CutMix) can be applied identically to the image and the cached depth map, so the target stays consistent. With metric depth, a resize must also rescale the intrinsics fed to FiLM/ray, or the scale becomes inconsistent. Prediction for this setup: long schedules (more than 40 epochs) only help if every epoch shows new geometric views. Without that, expect early overfitting on the 7.9k 'own' images, which fits the observed best-on-val-early behaviour. Evidence is from classification, so transfer to dense regression is inferred.
- Offline multi-crop / region-level teacher labels (cache the teacher on several random crops per image) [medium] — A Fast Knowledge Distillation Framework for Visual Recognition (FKD), Shen & Xing, ECCV 2022, arXiv:2112.01528 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.01528). Evidence: Region-level soft labels are generated once with the teacher run on random crops, stored with crop coordinates, and reused across epochs. The ECCV version reports ResNet-50 at 80.1% top-1 on ImageNet, +1.2 over ReLabel (global label map), and faster training than vanilla KD. The arXiv v1 reported 79.8%.. Inference cost: zero (train-only), plus a one-off offline teacher cost and storage of K crops per image. Applicability: This is the offline way to get crop-consistent metric teaching. A metric teacher's prediction on a crop (with adjusted intrinsics) is not the same as a crop of its full-image prediction, because scale cues change. Caching UniDepthV2 on K=4-8 random crops of each 'own' image (plus crop boxes and intrinsics) gives exact 'consistent teaching' targets for crop augmentation. It is also the principled replacement for s5/sced, which approximated this online and hurt in-domain val (s5 .101 vs .092). Classification evidence only.

- Supervise teacher pseudo-labels with scale-free losses (pairwise ranking plus SSI) and learn metric scale only from GT (detail/scale disentangling) [medium] — PatchRefiner: Leveraging Synthetic Data for Real-Domain High-Resolution Monocular Metric Depth Estimation, Li et al., ECCV 2024, arXiv:2406.06679 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06679). Evidence: Loss: L = L_silog(GT) + 0.1 L_rank(pseudo) + 0.1 L_ssi(pseudo).
