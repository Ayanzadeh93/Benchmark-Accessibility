# Fix the gauge, then teach scale

**Executive summary.** The best loss for the 5M-parameter b20dws student is the current loss with five measured defects repaired. Learning the per-image global scale is then the main lever, and only a few gated additions on the scale side are worth testing. The repairs are the squared-weight SILog moment, the hole-blind EG-SSI normaliser, the pad-contaminated scale gauge, the SILog lambda "tilt" under decoupling, and an aliased gradient term that is effectively switched off. On IBims, b20dws scores **raw d1 74.8 against 86.5 after a per-image median scale (d1_si)**, so perfect scale is worth **+11.7 d1**. The often-quoted 91.2 is d1_ssi, and the true headroom is the smaller figure. Structure regressed from b10 on every set (**d1_si −0.8 to −1.0**). Twelve candidate changes were merged from three design proposals and then checked adversarially for both novelty and technical correctness. **None survived as genuinely novel.** Every component has prior art, and the strongest ideas are incremental at best. Examples: zoom-equivariant scale is close to UniDepthV2's geometric-invariance loss and EFT-S in "Zoom In, Scale Out", and the delta-profile scale target is a RAML payoff distribution. Most expected gains are also smaller than the proposers claimed and sit inside IBims-100 seed noise (paired-bootstrap CI about ±4.9 d1). The recommended plan is therefore three steps. (1) A zero-cost "b20dws-fixed" baseline A0'. (2) Nine free diagnostics that decide which scale terms get GPU time. (3) Four gated, incremental scale-side terms. The most defensible paper contribution among them is a label-exact paired-view relative-scale loss that cancels teacher scale bias. The analysis itself is equally publishable: under decoupling, pseudo-label noise collapses to one scalar per image, and lambda < 1 becomes a structure-tilt force. Nothing in this report has been trained; every effect size is an estimate.

## The current loss spends most of its weight where the error is not

b20dws trains with `loss = (L_pix + L_s)/3`. Here `L_pix = 1.0·SILog(λ .85) + 0.5·proxSILog + 0.5·GradMatch(4 scales) + 0.3·EG-SSI`, all applied to the median-aligned prediction `p_al = U(r_c) + c + s*`, where `s*` is under stop-gradient. The other term is `L_s = SmoothL1_{β=.05}(ŝ − s*)`. The epoch-39 running averages show where the weight goes. **SILog carries 37% of the loss, EG-SSI 28%, prox-SILog 16%, the scale head 18%, and gradient matching only 2%** (0.0055 of 0.307). The scale head still underfits in-sample: **train s_hat_err 0.074 against 0.127 on IBims**. About 10% of all training pixels are synthetic pad ([codebase audit](<../research_notes/Loss design for tiny depth student/codebase_audit.md>)). The gain from b10 to b20dws is **entirely scale**: per-image IBims scale error falls 17.4 → 12.7. In-domain scale error is unchanged (NYU 7.14 → 7.06). The NYU −1.3 / SUN −0.4 raw-d1 regression is therefore a structure regression, and b20core shows the same pattern, which clears the dws4 blocks ([codebase audit](<../research_notes/Loss design for tiny depth student/codebase_audit.md>)).

The table below gives each term's role and the defect verification found in it. Each defect is real, but the verification pass corrected one important attribution. The squared-weight SILog moment and the hole-blind EG-SSI normaliser **were already present in b10**, so they cannot explain the b10 → b20dws structure regression. Only the lambda tilt and the pad gauge are new under decoupling. The likelier confounds are the untested bundle {trim, fov uncrop, ownar, cstm}, whose leave-one-out ladder was never run ([codebase audit](<../research_notes/Loss design for tiny depth student/codebase_audit.md>)).

| Term (share) | What it does under dsh | Verified defect or redundancy |
|---|---|---|
| SILog, λ .85 (37%) | Log-variance of the aligned residual, plus a `(1−λ)μ_w²` mean term | `diff = (p−t)·w` is squared, so `μ₂ = Σw²e²/Σw` (losses.py:43,46). With `range_weight_alpha = 1`, `w ∈ [1,2]`, so the moment is inconsistent and over-weights near pixels about 4:1. A fix is already documented in `distill/v2/losses.py` and `configs/v2/tier1/R1a.yaml` but has never been run. Under a median-aligned decoupled scale, `(1−λ)μ_w²` can be reduced only by tilting near against far, which is a spurious structure force. Its magnitude is small, about 1–2% of the gradient. |
| prox-SILog (16%) | Near band (< 2 m) up-weighted on aligned residuals; shapes structure only | Weights are applied once, so it is correct. Combined with the range weight, near pixels carry about 6× the weight of far ones. Its +5.1 IBims gain (S16) came in an *entangled* model. |
| Gradient matching (2%) | Multi-scale L1 on residual gradients; shift-invariant | Uses `::2` striding, which aliases, and pools over the batch, not per image. It starts at stride 1, yet the output is a bilinear upsample from stride 4. An L1 at stride 1 therefore prefers a flat ramp to a reproduced step edge (cost 1.5J vs J). Loss-value share is not gradient share, because the L1 sign-gradient is O(1/N). |
| EG-SSI (28%) | Median/MAD-normalised L1 weighted by target Sobel edges | Holes carry `log d = 0`, so their borders set `emax` (8.9/9.8 against 3.9/4.5 interior) in **85% of NYU and 82% of SUN images**. Real edges get squashed toward the 0.2 floor. MAD normalisation also makes it blind to log-range compression. |
| L_s (18%) | SmoothL1 to the stop-gradient median scale | `r_c` is centred over all pixels, pad included (student.py:295), so `s*` carries `a·(mean_pad r − mean_valid r)` target noise. GAP inputs pass through batch-4 BatchNorm, which can couple ŝ across images (untested). Under AdamW, the L_s weight scales only the trunk coupling, because Adam normalises the gradient of the head's own parameters. |
| Trim (own rows, top 10%) | Drops the largest aligned residuals from epoch 1 | It removes depth-extreme and edge pixels, exactly where range and gradient terms act. Its effect on structure has never been ablated. |

Two further defects matter for any later ablation. First, the existing `dsh_detach` path detaches the whole concatenated scale-head input, `k_embed` included, so `k_embed` stays at random init. A γ = 0 arm is invalid until only the three GAP taps are detached. Second, SILog runs on fp16 `p_al` under autocast. At λ = 1 the expression `μ₂ − μ²` is cancellation-prone, so it should be computed as a two-pass fp32 variance ([codebase audit](<../research_notes/Loss design for tiny depth student/codebase_audit.md>)). The literature backs the overall picture. MoGe-2 shows that a decoupled global-MLP scale head lifts metric d1 from 68.6 to 72.8 and also improves relative geometry ([MoGe-2](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.02546v1)). HDN shows that a *global* invariant auxiliary on top of a metric loss adds nothing (NYU δ1 80.1 → 79.1), while *hierarchical* contexts add +2.7 δ1 ([HDN](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2210.09670)). So b20dws already has the right decomposition. What it lacks is correct estimators inside it, plus more and better supervision for the one scalar that dominates unseen-scene error.

## Twelve candidates, re-ranked after adversarial checks

Each merged candidate went through a novelty check and a technical check against the code. The ranking below is the result, ordered by expected accuracy gain × strength of evidence. Zero-novelty fixes for measured defects rank first, because they are prerequisites for every later measurement. The original merge ranked the zoom-equivariance term (M2) second, as the headline novelty. Verification demoted it. Every training row already has a dense label, so its label-free branch is never exercised, and no unlabelled frame pool exists in the offline caches. Its expected gain shrank to +0 to +1.5 IBims d1. The gradient-coupling knob (M3) and the scale-loss sweep (M10) were found to be nearly the same experiment under AdamW, so they are run together. The near-field hinge (M12) was dropped: algebra bounds its effect to about a 1% log shift, and b20dws already has OCMR bias_log +0.002.

## No candidate is novel; two are defensibly incremental

Applying the strict rule (never call something novel if verification found prior art), **the novelty column contains no "novel" entry.** The verification panel's corrections should shape the paper.

The zoom-equivariant scale law was the planned headline. It is the median, scalar projection of UniDepthV2's dense geometric-invariance consistency, applied after camera conditioning. That loss is worth about +0.8 δ1 at ViT-S ([UniDepthV2](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.20110)). The closest prior art is "Zoom In, Scale Out". It already has the digital-zoom law, a teacher-anchored zoom-consistency fine-tune, the down-up anti-blur term, a scale-only MLP variant (EFT-S, α 0.03–0.09), and the self-target drift ablation. It also reports that equivariance fine-tuning *cost* unseen accuracy: KITTI AbsRel 0.138 → 0.175 and DIODE-out 0.731 → 1.000 ([Zoom In, Scale Out](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17903)). The same paper finds that Metric3D v2 with the true camera is already near-equivariant (α −0.07). b20dws conditions on K in the same canonical-focal way, so its baseline α may already be close to zero, and the "α → 0" figure would vanish. Verification also corrected the law itself. Zooming in by z raises c by log z, so ŝ must *fall*: `s₂ − s₁ = −log z + med_Ω(U₁_warp − U₂)`. The form `−log z + med(U₁_warp) − med(U₂)` is wrong, because the median is not linear.

The one element that survives with a defensible incremental claim is **L_rel**. Both scale targets come from the *same* label, so any per-image global-scale error in the V2-L pseudo-label cancels exactly in `s*₂ − s*₁`. That gives relative-scale supervision free of teacher bias on 40% of the mix. The claim is that this specific bias-cancelling difference target on a decoupled head is unpublished. It still has to beat a matched-compute "extra exact-K crop through L_s" control (Batch Augmentation), or reviewers will read it as more scale samples.

The delta-profile scale target (APD, renamed CPSD) is a RAML payoff distribution `q ∝ exp(reward/T)` with reward equal to oracle δ accuracy ([RAML](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.00150)), over log-scale bins as in ScaleNet ([ScaleNet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.04846)). Its only new element is that the payoff is computed per image from the dense residual field rather than from a kernel around a scalar label. Its "entropy as scale confidence" sub-claim is already covered by bin-entropy uncertainty work and by Scalix's global scale-uncertainty head. The image-level scale contrast (M8) is already in TR2M v2's coarse term, which pools each image to one embedding, sorts by pseudo scale and pulls similar-scale scenes together at weight 0.1 ([TR2M v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.13387v2)). The band-calibration term (RSA, renamed DSOC) is a depth-bin calibration regulariser from the imbalanced-regression literature. Those methods show the tail-versus-bulk trade-off on NYUD2-DIR: LDS raises few-shot δ1 0.570 → 0.630 but cuts many-shot δ1 0.777 → 0.701 ([DIR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.09554)).

The paper can therefore make only two kinds of claim. The first is an **analysis contribution**. Under a decoupled, median-aligned scale, (i) SILog's λ < 1 term turns into a structure-tilt penalty, which is a one-line corollary of Eigen's `(1−λ)μ²` decomposition ([Eigen 2014](https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.2283)), and (ii) pseudo-label scale noise becomes a separable scalar problem that can be measured and rectified on GT. On top of that comes a measured audit of α, blur sensitivity, BN coupling and per-source scale error for a 5M K-conditioned student. The second is **one or two incremental method components**: L_rel, and CPSD if it beats HL-Gauss centred on the profile argmax by at least 0.3 IBims d1. 2611.17903 must be cited as the closest prior art for the zoom component. RAML, SORD and ScaleNet must be cited for CPSD, and TR2M v2 for any contrastive scale term. The claim that "the optimal trunk coupling γ* shrinks with model size" needs γ sweeps at two or more capacities, for example B1 at 5M and the repo's 15.5M B2. Task-interference dependence on capacity is itself known ([Standley et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.07553)), so even that figure is incremental.

## The recommended loss: a clean core plus four gated scale terms

The recommendation separates an **evidence-backed core**, which every later run is measured against, from **gated incremental add-ons**. Each add-on enters the stack only if its diagnostic passes and its 15-epoch screen clears the acceptance rule. The core keeps the b20dws structure and weights, so in-domain accuracy is not put at risk. The core changes only estimators and gauges, and none of them reaches inference: the forward graph and ONNX export are bit-identical at pad = None.

### Evidence-backed core (A0', "b20dws-fixed")

```
Notation (training):
  m4   = 1 − avgpool4(pad)                                  # soft non-pad fraction at stride 4
  r_c  = r − Σ(m4·r) / max(Σ m4, 1)                         # fp32; equals mean_hw(r) when pad = None
  U    = bilinear upsample to full resolution;  c = log(f_px / 518.9)
  s*_b = nanmedian_{valid_b}( logD − U(r_c) − c )           # fp32, no_grad, pre-trim valid, pad excluded, ok_b iff ≥500 px
  p_al = U(r_c) + c + s*                                    # s* stop-grad
  e    = p_al.float() − logD

Structure:
  SILog_v(e)  = sqrt( Σ v(e − μ)² / Σ v  +  (1 − λ) μ²  +  1e-6 ),   μ = Σ v e / Σ v,   λ = 1
                v = eff_mask² (arm a2, default: keeps the legacy 4:1 near emphasis, consistently)
                v = eff_mask  (arm a1: weights once, 2:1)
  L_EG        = EG-SSI with  edge ← (|S_x t| + |S_y t|) · (1 − maxpool3x3(~valid_pretrim)),
                edge_w = 0.2 + 0.8 · clamp(edge / q99(edge), 0, 1)        # fp32; replaces the separate pad path
  L_grad4     = native-stride gradient matching (strides 4, 8, 16, 32; masked avg-pool; per-image; untrimmed mask)

  L_pix = 1.0·SILog_v  +  0.5·proxSILog_v(λ = 1)  +  λ_g·L_grad4  +  0.3·L_EG
          λ_g chosen so that ||∂(λ_g L_grad4)/∂r_c|| / ||∂SILog/∂r_c|| = 0.25 on a fixed probe batch

Scale:
  g      = cat( GradScale_γ( GAP F4, GAP F3, GAP x_td ),  k_embed(k) )   # k_embed always gets the full gradient
  ŝ      = scale_mlp(g)
  s_t    = s* − ā·1[own]                                     # M7-S0 rectifier, ā per-sensor from the GT calibration split
  L_s    = mean_{b: ok_b} SmoothL1_β( ŝ_b − s_t,b ),   β = 0.05 (until the M10' sweep says otherwise)

L_core = ( L_pix + 1.0·L_s ) / 3        # AdamW, GradScaler, clip 1.0, EMA .999, cosine, as today
         γ = 1 until the M3/M10' sweep; trim own rows 10% from epoch 1 (epoch 3 tested as leave-one-out)
```

Every element of the core has either a confirmed defect behind it or a published estimator behind it. Arm a2 is the default because it removes the inconsistency without silently reweighting the near band, which is the safety-relevant range. Arm a1 is the ablation. The pre-trim `valid` must be passed explicitly into the EG-SSI mask. Using `target_log == 0` is wrong because NYU pixels at exactly 1.000 m have log 0. Using the trimmed mask is wrong because trimmed pixels are not holes. Dropping the stride-1 and stride-2 levels of gradient matching follows directly from the representability argument: the output is bilinear from stride 4. The weight comes from a gradient-norm ratio rather than a loss-value share. ā corrects the measured V2-family over-range of **+0.021 to +0.026 log** ([codebase audit](<../research_notes/Loss design for tiny depth student/codebase_audit.md>)). It is a constant per sensor and can be evaluated as its own arm at zero training cost.

### Gated incremental add-ons, in stacking order

```
L_total = ( L_pix  +  L_scale ) / 3

L_scale = 1.0·L_s(s_t')                                  # s_t' = target after (A) or (B)
        + 0.3·KL( q_b ‖ softmax(z_b.float()) )            # (A) CPSD sibling head, train-only     [gate D1]
        + 0.3·L_pv + 0.3·L_rel                            # (C) paired-view, label-exact          [gate D2]
        + 0.05·L_RnC                                      # (E) ordinal latent, XBM queue         [gate D8]
L_pix  += 0.15·L_DSOC                                     # (D) band calibration, Bland-Altman axis [gate D7]

(A) CPSD:  s_t' = ½·s_t + ½·E_q[b]           (V3 readout; inference unchanged)
(B) DCST (alternative to A, never both):  s_t' = s_t + a_t·clip(s_mode − s*, −0.1, 0.1),  a_t: 0 → 0.5 over epochs 5–20
(C) L_pv  = SmoothL1_.05( ŝ₂ − s*₂ );   L_rel = SmoothL1_.05( (ŝ₂ − sg ŝ₁) − (s*₂ − s*₁) ),  p = 0.5 per step, ramp from epoch 3
All scale add-ons pass through the same GradScale_γ*; ramp each linearly over its first 3–5 epochs.
```

The ordering reflects what verification found about each term's mechanism. CPSD and DCST move the *training target* toward the per-image δ-optimal scale. They share an optimum, so they are run head-to-head, and only the winner is kept. Both must beat two controls: HL-Gauss centred on the profile argmax, and an effective-weight control with the L_s weight at 1.5. PVS adds scale supervision with the teacher's bias cancelled. DSOC and RnC are the least likely to help IBims and come last. The weights above are the verification-refined values: 0.3 for KL rather than 0.5, 0.05 for RnC because the clip budget is shared, and 0.15 for DSOC within its 0.1–0.2 safe band. The γ re-sweep over {0.1, 1} happens once, on the full stack. A 2026 frozen-backbone study offers indirect support for a scalar target term combined with a dense metric term: removing its oracle-scale regression cost about 1.1 NYU δ1 ([Language as Prior](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.01457)). That is evidence for keeping L_s as the convex anchor under every add-on, not for any particular add-on.

## Free diagnostics decide half the plan before any GPU hour

Most go/no-go decisions can be made on the existing b20dws checkpoint and the results_bench JSON without training. Running them first is the single most efficient step. Each gate is pre-registered:

| ID | Diagnostic (no training) | Gates | Proceed if |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | Oracle IBims/NYU/SUN d1, AbsRel and MAE under median, profile-argmax and window-mode alignment; distribution of \|s_mode − s*\| on train caches after trim | CPSD, DCST | mode/argmax beats median by ≥ 0.3 aligned d1, AbsRel/MAE cost ≤ 1%, median \|Δ\| ≥ 0.01 |
| D2 | Zoom exponent α at z ∈ {1.25, 1.5, 2} and out of range {2.5, 3, 0.8, 0.67}, centred and off-centre; blur sensitivity at b ∈ {1.5, 2} | PVS self-consistency part | \|α_in\| > 0.15 or blur > 3%; otherwise keep only the label-exact L_pv/L_rel |
| D3 | s_hat_err on about 500 un-augmented training images, eval-mode BN vs train-mode BN (snapshot and restore buffers), per source | PreciseBN-freeze | gap > 0.02 log |
| D4 | Per-source s_hat_err (GT vs own) | M7 priority | own ≫ GT supports target-side work |
| D5 | Per-group gradient-norm ratio \|\|∂L_s/∂θ\|\| / \|\|∂L_pix/∂θ\|\| and clip-fire rate | γ range | sets the γ / λ_t grid |
| D6 | Teacher-to-GT scale error e_L on the SUN calibration split; R² and Spearman of disagreement with \|e_L\| (needs a teacher pass) | M7-S1/S2 | CV RMSE drop ≥ 10% and ≥ 0.005 log |
| D7 | Median residual per 5 depth bins, binned by GT, by prediction, and by Bland-Altman average | DSOC | pred- or average-binned bias > 0.03 log in the near or far bin |
| D8 | IBims mean(ŝ − s*) vs std(ŝ − s*); Spearman(ŝ, s*) on IBims vs in-domain | M8' | std ≥ 60% of RMS and IBims rank correlation clearly below in-domain |
| D9 | Shrinkage slope of signed log-bias against (m_gt − mean_train m); Spearman(p̃, \|bias\|) | M11 | slope CI excludes 0, or rho < −0.2 |

The training schedule follows from those gates. **Phase 0** runs D1–D5 and D7–D9 (hours, no GPU training), plus D6 if the teacher pass is approved. **Phase 1** compares A0 (b20dws) with A0' (all fixes, arm a2) and A0' with a1, using 3 seeds × 40 epochs. It adds leave-one-out over fixes a–d (2 seeds × 15 epochs) and the never-run b20 ladder (−trim, −fov, −ownar, −cstm, 2 seeds × 15 epochs). That ladder is the only experiment that can attribute the b10 → b20dws structure regression, which the fix-pack alone cannot explain. **Phase 2** runs the coupling and operating-point sweep on A0'. That means λ_t ∈ {0 (corrected dsh_detach), 0.5, 1, 2} and the distance arms β ∈ {0.02, 0.05, 0.25}, L2-log and own-row Cauchy, run one factor at a time (about 9 screens), plus PreciseBN-freeze if D3 passes. **Phase 3** runs M7-S0, then CPSD-V3 against DCST against both controls, then PVS against its matched-compute controls. Those controls are wider exact-K augmentation alone, L_pv alone, and L_pv + L_rel. **Phase 4** runs M9's native-stride gradient and EG-SSI weight sweep, then DSOC (avg, pred and gt axes; the gt axis is the documented negative control), M8' and M11 if their gates pass. **Phase 5** stacks the survivors, runs the final γ re-sweep {0.1, 1}, and confirms with 3 seeds × 40 epochs.

Statistics and selection follow the verification's warnings. IBims-1 has n = 100. The paired-bootstrap CI for b20dws against b10 was about ±4.9 d1, so aggregate d1 cannot resolve a +0.5 effect. The primary readouts are therefore per-image paired statistics: a per-image paired bootstrap (10k resamples) of d1 and AbsRel on IBims, NYU-654 and SUN, and a Wilcoxon signed-rank test on per-image \|ŝ − s*\|. The failure rate (fraction of images with \|ŝ − s*\| > 0.223) and the scale-error MAD are reported alongside. Operating points are selected on own-val (AbsRel 0.182 against V2-L, never trained on, OCMR-like) and NYU/SUN val, never on IBims. If IBims is used to screen, the paper must say so. NYU/SUN val is a random 3% of *training* frames and shares scenes with training ([codebase audit](<../research_notes/Loss design for tiny depth student/codebase_audit.md>)).

A component is kept only if four conditions hold. Mean IBims raw d1 over seeds is non-negative with the per-image test favouring it. NYU d1 and d1_si each fall by no more than 0.2–0.3. The effect has the same sign in every seed. Its mechanism metric moves as predicted: for example, aligned d1 stays flat for scale-only terms, and held-out s* RMSE falls for M7. A component stops early if its 15-epoch screen loses more than 0.5 NYU d1_si or raises the clip-fire rate above 30%. Every row reports:

- IBims raw d1, AbsRel, RMSE and MAE, with d1_si and d1_ssi;
- per-source median and MAD of ŝ − s*;
- AbsRel per range bin (0–1, 1–2, 2–4, 4–8, > 8 m), binned by both GT and prediction;
- α;
- NYU and SUN d1 and d1_si;
- OCMR objAbsRel and the near-band over-estimation rate.

## Where this plan can be wrong

The most important caveat is that **no candidate has been trained**. Every gain in the tables is an estimate from verification or analogy, and the estimates do not add up. Realistically, the fix-pack recovers a fraction of a point in-domain. If the scale-side add-ons pass their gates, together they plausibly yield +1 to +3 IBims raw d1 against an +11.7 ceiling. Much of that may be shared between CPSD/DCST and PVS. The 11.7-point headroom is a generalisation gap of the scale head on unseen scenes (train error 0.074 against IBims 0.127), and target shaping on training images addresses it only indirectly.

The literature points to a larger lever that is not a loss change at all: **more scale-labelled images**. Unlabelled in-domain data moved a 1.7M depth student from d1 0.808 to 0.837 ([Hu et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2105.06143)) and a 2.7M structured-KD student from rel 0.175 to 0.160 ([SKD](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1903.04197)). Verification judged that cached V2-L pseudo-labels on new K-known indoor frames would dominate any equivariance target on the same frames. Such a pool does not exist on disk today. Users who care most about unseen-scene accuracy should weigh building it against the loss work below the fix-pack.

Several specific risks remain open. The V2 teachers share training data and architecture, so a multi-teacher scale posterior is likely uninformative without a non-V2 teacher, and new teacher passes break the offline-cache constraint. SUN-RGBD contains NYUv2 frames and four sensors, so the M7 calibration split needs de-duplication and per-sensor intercepts. Direct sub-10M evidence for every structure loss here (HDN, EG-SSI, local normalisation) is absent: the published gains come from 22M–300M backbones ([HDN](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2210.09670), [UniDepthV2](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.20110)). The repo's own history says auxiliary losses were neutral for small students on real GT. Some cited figures rest on partially verified extractions, including the DA3 teacher-label table and the TR2M coarse-versus-fine split, which TR2M does not ablate. Finally, the "the near band is compressed" premise behind M6 and M12 was measured on an older B1 student, binned by true distance. It is most likely regression dilution rather than a correctable slope, and D7 exists to settle it before any band loss is trained.

## Conclusion

Verification turned the brief's premise around. A tiny student does need its loss to be "perfect", but for this model perfection means *consistent estimators and gauges*, not more terms. The five repairs are free and remove real inconsistencies between the alignment, the weighting and the pad gauge. They also make every later scale ablation interpretable, which a noisy s* and a randomly initialised k_embed currently do not. Beyond them, the honest ceiling for loss-only changes is a few IBims points. The decisive evidence comes from cheap diagnostics: α, the BN train/eval gap, the gap between oracle median and mode, and the band bias by prediction against by GT. More 40-epoch runs will not supply it.

For the paper, the sharpest defensible story is analytical rather than a new loss. Decoupling the scale reduces pseudo-label noise and teacher bias to a single scalar per image, which can be measured, rectified on GT and cancelled across paired views. Leftover SILog mean terms become tilt forces on structure. The incremental components (L_rel, and CPSD if it beats its argmax control) are the instruments that demonstrate this. They are not the contribution, and the paper should cite UniDepthV2, "Zoom In, Scale Out", RAML and TR2M v2 as the ground it stands on.
